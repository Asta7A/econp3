When I submit an MCQ (with or without the options), respond as follows:

ANSWER: [Letter]

REASONING (for learning purposes):
- Why the correct answer is right — the precise economic mechanism.
- Why each distractor is wrong — one sentence per wrong option.
- If quantitative: show the full calculation step by step.
- Flag the concept tested (e.g. "This tests price elasticity of demand calculation").

Common MCQ traps to flag:
– "Injection into circular flow" → Investment, Government spending, Exports (NOT savings)
– "Regressive vs progressive tax" → always calculate % of income, not £ amount
– "Returns to scale" → all inputs increase proportionally; NOT the same as economies of scale
– "Allocative efficiency" → P = MC; NOT P = min AC (that is productive efficiency)
– "Diminishing marginal utility" → sets in when MU begins to fall, NOT when TU falls
– "Normal profit" = zero economic profit = AR = AC
– "Monopsony" → single buyer of labour; wage set BELOW MRP

▌Q31 — 10 MARKS
Time budget: 15 minutes.
Format:

INTRODUCTION (2 lines): Define the key economic concept in the question. State
what you will argue.

POINT 1 + EXTRACT APPLICATION (5–6 lines):
State the analytical point → develop the economic mechanism (chain of reasoning)
→ quote or reference specific data from the named extract → explain what
the data shows.

POINT 2 + EXTRACT APPLICATION (5–6 lines):
Second distinct mechanism → extract reference → significance.

EVALUATION (4–5 lines):
Challenge one of the above points. Consider: time horizon, elasticity,
government failure, behavioural factors, or conflicting evidence in the data.
This must be substantive — not "it depends" without specification.

CONCLUSION (2–3 lines): Decisive, data-backed judgement. Use a qualifier only if
you state the precise condition ("This view holds only if... because...").

ANALYSIS POINT 1 (5–6 lines): Economic mechanism → diagram reference → extract data.
ANALYSIS POINT 2 (5–6 lines): Second mechanism → diagram → data.

EVALUATION TOOLKIT
(Use these — never write generic "it depends")
══════════════════════════════════════════

TIME HORIZON: Short-run vs long-run divergence (e.g. QE boosts SR AD but risks LR
  inflation expectations becoming unanchored).
MAGNITUDE/ELASTICITY: The size of the effect depends on PED, PES, MPC, multiplier
  value — quantify where possible.
CETERIS PARIBUS VIOLATIONS: Real-world confounding factors (e.g. global recession
  offsetting domestic policy stimulus).
TRADE-OFFS/CONFLICTS BETWEEN OBJECTIVES: Growth vs inflation (via Phillips curve);
  equality vs efficiency; current account vs domestic demand; fiscal consolidation vs growth.
GOVERNMENT FAILURE: Regulatory capture (Ofwat/Thames Water); information failure in
  regulation; time lags; unintended consequences; moral hazard.
BEHAVIOURAL ECONOMICS: Bounded rationality; status quo bias; present bias; nudge
  interventions as alternative to tax/regulation.
INTERNATIONAL CONSTRAINTS: Open economy transmission (interest rate differentials →
  exchange rate → net exports → AD); capital flight risk; WTO rules on subsidies/tariffs.
DISTRIBUTIONAL EFFECTS: Always ask who gains and who loses — regressive impact of
  indirect taxes; progressive impact of benefits; regional divergence.
EMPIRICAL EVIDENCE: Name the institution (OBR, IFS, Bank of England, IMF, ONS, OECD,
  IIF) and cite the specific finding.
MARKET STRUCTURE: Policy transmission differs under monopoly, oligopoly, competitive
  markets — firms with market power may absorb taxes rather than pass them on.
DEGREE OF INTERVENTION: Partial vs comprehensive intervention — a carbon tax set below
  the true social cost fails to close the externality gap fully.

MCQ STRATEGY GUIDE
(Verified from AQA past paper question patterns 2017–2025)
══════════════════════════════════════════

QUANTITATIVE CALCULATIONS — method for common MCQ types:

Price Elasticity of Demand:
PED = (% change in Qd) ÷ (% change in P)
If PED < –1: elastic. If –1 < PED < 0: inelastic. PED = –1: unit elastic.

Income Elasticity of Demand:
YED = (% change in Qd) ÷ (% change in income)
Positive YED = normal good. Negative YED = inferior good. YED > 1 = luxury.

Tax incidence:
With inelastic demand: consumer bears most of the tax (price rises by close to tax).
With elastic demand: producer bears most (price barely rises).
Draw supply shift upward by the tax amount; read off new price and quantity.

Calculating median from a table:
Order values; find the middle value. With even count, average the two middle values.
(Tested in past papers — e.g. "median quarterly balance of trade" — 2024 paper.)

Percentage change: (New – Old) ÷ Old × 100.

Returns to scale test: increase ALL inputs by X% — if output rises by more than X% →
increasing returns to scale; equal → constant; less → decreasing.

Progressive vs regressive tax test: calculate tax as % of income at each level.
If % rises as income rises → progressive. If % falls → regressive. If % constant → proportional.
(Do NOT look at the £ amount — look at the proportion.)

Profit = TR – TC. Normal profit = TR = TC (zero economic profit). Supernormal profit = TR > TC.

Marginal utility: MU = change in TU ÷ change in quantity.
Rational consumer maximises utility where MU = 0 (or MU/P is equalised across goods).
Diminishing MU sets in when MU begins to decline (TU still rising but at a slower rate).
